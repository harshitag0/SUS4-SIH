# Business viability & differentiation

## What incumbents already do (e.g. Sahyadri Farms, Nashik)
Large FPO-company model: aggregates thousands of member farmers, runs packhouses, cold chain and processing, follows export-grade compliance, tracks produce from farm to lot, and holds direct contracts with big retailers and exporters. Strengths: scale, infrastructure, brand, working capital.
*(General knowledge, not live research — verify specifics from Sahyadri's own materials before pitching.)*

## Where a new platform can still win
| Gap | AgriLink angle |
|---|---|
| Small/mid FPOs can't reach importers or judge price | Open requirement board + price benchmark vs mandi |
| Prices negotiated opaquely | Forecast fair price shown to both sides; escalation clauses |
| Verbal deals collapse when prices move | Digital bond, escrow split, tamper-evident record |
| Quality disputes at destination | Stage certificates + consultant-verified grading + automatic grade-based price adjustment |
| Data silos per FPO | Cross-FPO demand/supply forecast and reliability scores |

Positioning: **not a rival FPO but the trade-and-trust layer FPOs use.** Large FPOs can be customers too.

## Revenue options
1. SaaS per FPO (dashboards, forecasting, certificates).
2. Transaction fee (1-2%) on bonds executed.
3. Quality-certification / consultant-visit fee.
4. Financing or insurance referral (pre-harvest advance against an active bond).

## Risks to validate
- Will importers post requirements on a platform instead of calling known suppliers? (Interview ~10 Singapore importers.)
- Real photo-grading accuracy on orchards; consultant cost per visit.
- Regulation: cross-border payments/escrow, e-signature validity, export compliance.
- Alphonso season is ~10 weeks: other fruit and corridors are needed for year-round retention.

## Scale path
Alphonso -> Kesar / Totapuri -> pomegranate, grapes, banana -> other corridors (UAE, UK, Netherlands). The engines are crop-agnostic; only grade specs and price series change.
